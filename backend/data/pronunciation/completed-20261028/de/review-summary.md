# German pronunciation review

All 500 actual Lexora German entries (IDs 18361–18860) were reviewed with their
existing parts of speech and Russian meanings. Exact identities were preserved.

- Result: **500 ready, 0 held** approximate Cyrillic reading aids.
- Resolved all 81 original review flags; 50 proposed readings were corrected.
- Reviewed the complete 500-row list, not only the flagged rows.
- Used targeted Duden and IDS pronunciation/stress references and a broader
  Duden pronunciation-markup cross-check of longer dictionary entries.
- A separate vowel-nucleus/stress-position scan agrees with available Duden
  stress marks. Telefon deliberately retains the accepted final-stress variant;
  Duden also lists an initial-stress variant, explained in its note.
- Native-audio listening/verifications: **0**. These are approximate learning
  aids, not IPA, native recordings, or a claim of phonetic certification.

Important corrections include willkommen, Karotte, Apotheke, Bibliothek, Hotel,
Rabatt, Büro, Tastatur, Notizbuch and Unterrichtsstunde stress; German readings of
T-Shirt, E-Mail, Computer, Laptop and Jeans; Orange's consonant; and compound
boundaries in Mittagessen/Abendessen. No new meanings or entries were introduced.

The output retains the established `russian-cyrillic-v1` notation. Individual
notes explain ö/ü without initial й, ich-/ach-Laut, the single velar nasal ng,
vocalized r, phrase stress and useful loanword exceptions. Long-vowel contrasts
are not fully encoded by ordinary Cyrillic; IPA/audio remain the precision
reference. Notes do not claim that automatic eSpeak output proves accuracy.

Files: `de-reviewed.jsonl`, `de-review-decisions.json` (all changes and resolved
flags plus primary-reference observations). Original source files stay intact.
