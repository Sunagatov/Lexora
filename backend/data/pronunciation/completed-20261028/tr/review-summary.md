# Remaining Turkish pronunciation guides

Reviewed exactly the **116 previously held Turkish Word IDs** against their
existing terms, meanings, parts of speech, supplied reading proposals and
contextual dictionary material. No existing 384 imported hints were edited.

`tr-resolved.json` supplies 116 approximate reading guides, `status=ready`,
`issue=null`, a per-ID reasoning record and fresh learner-facing notes. All
workflow boilerplate about not importing before native review was replaced by
actual sound/stress guidance. This is not a claim of native-audio certification.

## Explicit remaining precision limits

Fifteen guides deliberately do not assert a stress mark where references are
incomplete or conflict: büyükanne, büyükbaba, kâse, öğle yemeği, elbise, düğme,
mide, havalimanı, cümle, tablo, mavi, burada, orada, içeride and dışarıda.
Each note states this plainly. Their stable segmental pronunciation is still
useful, and omitting an uncertain accent is preferable to teaching a guessed one.

Seven additional phrases mark secure/main component stress without forcing
every secondary accent: benim adım, akşam yemeği, hava durumu, yüzme havuzu,
fotoğraf makinesi, doğum günü and hadi gidelim.

Specific corrections include gece/geceler noun stress; noun çizme rather than
the same-spelled negative imperative; stressable noun case/possessive endings
in okula, bisiklete, yardıma and ihtiyacım; üniversite, şapka and palto lexical
stress; dizüstü's compound reading; negative continuous pre-accenting in
anlamıyorum/yemiyorum; unstressed question mi; and unstressed polite-imperative
-(y)In in konuşun/çağırın. The guide does not equate all personal, nominal and
negative suffixes under a single final-stress rule.

## References/method

- TDK-published Talat Tekin, *Türkçede Kelime Vurgusu Kuralları*:
  https://tdk.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/1062.-Makaleler-III-Cagdas-Turk-Dilleri.pdf
- TDK-published discussion of progressive and negative stress:
  https://tdk.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/1060.-Makaleler-I-Altayistik.pdf
- University teaching material distinguishing stressed noun possessives from
  unstressed predicative-person suffixes:
  https://faculty.sbs.arizona.edu/okal/CMES_Turkish-Global-Simulation/GREG_gramer_all.htm
- University Turkish Language Department stress notes, including negative,
  question and nominalization contrasts:
  https://www.ktu.edu.tr/turkdili/guzdonemidersicerikleri
- Official RTÜK *Türkçe Telaffuz Sözlüğü*, Serhat Demirel (2021):
  https://arsivdosya.rtuk.gov.tr/assets/Icerik/BilgiMerkezi/TeleffuzSozlugu/telaffuz-sozlugu-2202.pdf
  Its vowel length/soft-ğ guidance was checked visually and textually. **Page 14
  explicitly says stress underlining is selective. An unmarked entry does not
  establish final stress.** Its acute é means closed e, not an accent mark.
- Contextual Wiktionary IPA extracts supplied by the root review, through Kaikki:
  https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Turkish/index.html
  These support meanings/readings but their generated stress is not accepted
  blindly: the explicit TDK MAsa exception takes precedence over a default final
  accent in a dictionary template.
- Supplementary Turkish-language stress guidance (imperative/person suffixes):
  https://www.turkedebiyati.org/vurgu-ve-cesitleri/

All examples are learner approximations for standard Turkey Turkish. ö/ü are
front rounded vowels without an invented initial й; written y remains a real
consonant. ı is distinct from i. Soft ğ is not a Russian hard г. Vowel length and
the detailed phrase intonation cannot be completely encoded in plain Cyrillic.

No external AI API calls or native-audio certification; no database, application
source, runtime settings, commits or deployment changed by this review.
